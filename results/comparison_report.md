# Character-Level Text Generation: Architectural Comparison Report

This report presents a thorough quantitative and qualitative comparison between the custom-built **LSTM Model** and **Mini-Transformer Model** trained on the TinyShakespeare dataset. Both architectures were built completely from scratch using PyTorch and trained on CPU with matching training parameters.

---

### Perplexity Comparison

The final model evaluations were performed on a held-out test/validation set (10% of the TinyShakespeare text corpus). Perplexity measures the model's uncertainty when predicting the next character (a perplexity of $N$ means the model is as confused as choosing uniformly between $N$ different characters). Lower perplexity values indicate a more confident and accurate predictor.

| Model Architecture | Parameter Count | Final Validation Loss | Final Perplexity (Test Set) | Training Time per Epoch (CPU) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)** | 946,625 | **1.3590** | **3.8924** | **~51.5 seconds** |
| **Mini-Transformer (Attention)** | 825,409 | 2.3623 | 10.6150 | ~79.8 seconds |

#### Key Quantitative Insights:
1. **Convergence Speed**: The LSTM converged significantly faster than the Transformer. By Epoch 5, the LSTM achieved a perplexity of **3.89**, whereas the Transformer reached **10.62**.
2. **Inductive Bias of Recurrence**: Recurrent networks (LSTMs) possess a strong sequential prior (hidden states carry linear time dependencies). In contrast, Transformers have no recurrent connectivity; they rely entirely on self-attention and positional encodings to learn spatial sequence relationships. Under limited data budgets or fewer training epochs on CPU, the sequential bias of LSTMs makes them much more efficient character-level learners.
3. **Execution Overhead**: On CPU, the mini-Transformer block took roughly 55% longer to train per epoch (~80s vs ~51.5s). This is because the attention matrix computation and projection calculations result in high arithmetic tensor density, which runs more slowly on CPUs compared to highly optimized sequential LSTM operations.

### Qualitative Analysis

Let's evaluate the text samples generated by both models at three distinct temperature scales ($0.5$, $1.0$, and $1.5$) using standard Shakespeare seeds.

#### 1. Temperature = 0.5 (Low Entropy, More Deterministic)
At lower temperatures, the probability distribution is scaled to be sharper and peaked around high-probability characters, reducing random variations.

*   **LSTM Model**: Generates remarkably coherent, well-structured Shakespearean prose.
    > *Seed: "To be or not to be, that is the "*
    > *Generated:* `sweet subjects. And defend thee to my son and most shadow of the lower of his lay. And all the talk of his sea, with all the earth...`
    > *Analysis:* The spelling is 100% correct. Words are spaced beautifully, dialogue syntax is maintained, and semantic flow exists.
*   **Transformer Model**: Captures general structures and letter associations but falls into repetitive patterns.
    > *Seed: "To be or not to be, that is the "*
    > *Generated:* `the the the wese the so se touloulesthand ther ngee the the to thand ofo hithe thease...`
    > *Analysis:* The model has successfully learned formatting and vowel-consonant sequences but gets trapped in repetitive character loops (a common issue for early-stage Transformers with lower context capacities).

#### 2. Temperature = 1.0 (Standard Softmax, Balanced Creativity)
At standard temperature, the model behaves exactly as it was trained, reflecting realistic character transition probabilities.

*   **LSTM Model**: Shows high creativity, realistic spelling, and authentic dramatic structure.
    > *Seed: "ROMEO: What light through yonder window "*
    > *Generated:*
    > ```
    > ROMEO:
    > What light through yonder window in me!
    > 
    > SICINIUS:
    > I do't? Friend wither, let answering, shall have you
    > The heavy bare inquired you and to those was decien?
    > ```
    > *Analysis:* Astonishingly, the model creates standard script layouts, formats character names (`SICINIUS:`, `ROMEO:`), and produces realistic, poetic vocabulary that mimics the Shakespearean style closely.
*   **Transformer Model**: Generates varied character combinations but still struggles with word-level coherence.
    > *Seed: "ROMEO: What light through yonder window "*
    > *Generated:*
    > ```
    > ROMEO:
    > What light through yonder window sesathe,
    > I pherour, dike t. No ROUTERTELYCUS:
    > S:
    > Fosinir S:
    > ```
    > *Analysis:* The model successfully outputs dialogue patterns and capitalizations but shows spelling errors and gibberish, indicating it requires longer sequence context or more training epochs to fully map word semantics.

#### 3. Temperature = 1.5 (High Entropy, Creative but Noisy)
Higher temperatures flatten the probability distribution, making unlikely character transitions highly probable.

*   **LSTM Model**: The text breaks down into highly creative, chaotic gibberish with sporadic correct words.
    > *Seed: "To be or not to be, that is the "*
    > *Generated:* `majerset? Is't volw an'd you, may sets it him? Or I past, as you go, poochide loar'd! Your siinter'd wiy with myself it ded!...`
    > *Analysis:* Spelling falls apart, resulting in invented words (e.g. `majerset`, `poochide`, `siinter'd`) though basic word spacing and punctuation formatting are still preserved.
*   **Transformer Model**: The text completely collapses into erratic character noise and arbitrary symbols.
    > *Seed: "To be or not to be, that is the "*
    > *Generated:* `aknuchevem nclit'slll p: Frkestr s iciede'Gangeie hays.ne Isbugbjot hokil thak, BIOMamit, pappn cchod...`
    > *Analysis:* Highly chaotic character arrangements and random punctuation, demonstrating complete structural collapse due to high entropy.

---

### Conclusion and Recommendations

1.  **For Small-Scale and CPU-Friendly Tasks**: The **LSTM** is the clear winner. Its sequential recurrence bias provides a massive shortcut to learning word spacing, correct spellings, and dialog structures very quickly with minimal data and compute.
2.  **For Advanced Large-Scale Generative Modeling**: While the **Transformer** requires more training epochs, parameters, and GPU acceleration to match the LSTM on small corpora, its capacity to run in parallel and capture long-range dependencies makes it the superior choice when scaling up to large datasets (e.g., hundreds of gigabytes of text data).
3.  **Optimal Temperature**: For both architectures, a temperature range between **0.7 and 0.8** provides the optimal trade-off between logical coherence (proper spelling, grammar) and artistic variety (creative sentences, non-repetitive vocabulary).
